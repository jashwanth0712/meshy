The whole product in one picture. Top: the grant itself. claude-1.db1.prod.acme.eth reads right to left as root, org, network, device, agent. That one ENSv2 subname is the permission: it expires on its own (1 hour by default), it is owned by the agent's address but registered with roleBitmap 0 so the agent cannot renew or move it, and its meshy.policy record holds the folder rules sealed to the device's X25519 key and the org key. Middle: the life of a grant. 01 Join: the agent runs /meshy:join, a human approves through World ID, and the agent gets claude-1.agents.acme.eth with its own resolver. 02 Grant: an admin registers the subname and seals the policy, bound to the grant's token id. 03 Work: meshyd sandboxes the session (bubblewrap plus GateFS on Linux, Seatbelt on macOS) to the granted folders, a command allowlist, or a single service port. No network inside. 04 Block: anything outside the grant, like /etc/shadow, ../ or a symlink out, returns MESHY_BLOCKED and is logged. 05 Revoke: unregister() on Sepolia. meshyd re-checks every live grant on every block, so the session is killed in the block that carries the revocation, about 150 ms after the daemon sees it. The dashed tail is the hour the agent never got to use. Bottom: every step lands on Arkiv as a read-only entity, written by the device's own address and sealed to the org key.
