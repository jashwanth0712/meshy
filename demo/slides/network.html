One org, one WireGuard mesh, five networks. Each blue platform is a network and its own ENSv2 registry: prod.acme.eth, staging.acme.eth, data.acme.eth, home.acme.eth and office.acme.eth. Each box on it is a device name, like db1.prod.acme.eth, and camera.pi.home.acme.eth floats above pi as a service published in its meshy.service record. The thin lavender lines on the floor are the WireGuard tunnels between networks; the mesh itself runs on NetBird's userspace client, operated by a staked provider. Around the edge are the people and agents. Solid blue lines are grants, and each one is its own expiring subname: claude-1 reads /srv/reports on db1, read-only, for one hour; analyst reads the warehouse for four hours; lens-bot gets one port on the camera for 30 minutes and never a shell. Each grant line climbs onto exactly one platform and ends at exactly one box: the agent sees nothing else. Dashed lines are admin roles: alice owns the org, and bob is a delegated admin of staging through Enhanced Access Control, so he can grant and revoke there but nowhere else. The grey agent has no World ID attestation and is refused before it reaches anything (MESHY_NOT_HUMAN_BACKED). The grey nas at the bottom left was never enrolled: it has no name and no meshyd, so nothing in the mesh can reach it. Honest status: in the live demo the NetBird mesh hasn't run on a provider yet, and humans act as wallet-holding admins; registering people's own devices as names is on the roadmap.
